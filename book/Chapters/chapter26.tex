\section{Reading Context}
\begin{readingbox}[title=Dialogue: Reviewing a Report]
\textbf{Editor:} I read your draft. It's good, but there are a few errors.\\
\textbf{Writer:} Really? I thought I checked it carefully.\\
\textbf{Editor:} You missed some articles. For example, you wrote "I have car" instead of "I have \textbf{a} car".\\
\textbf{Writer:} Oh, I see. I'm always worried \textbf{about} making mistakes with articles.\\
\textbf{Editor:} Don't worry. Also, use more linking words. \textbf{However}, the content is excellent.\\
\textbf{Writer:} Thanks. I'll give it another look. I'm interested \textbf{in} improving my writing.\\
\textbf{Editor:} Great. Remember to plan before you write next time.
\end{readingbox}

\section{Key Concepts: The Writing Process}

Good writing follows three key stages:

\begin{vocabbox}[title=The 3 Stages]
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \keyterm{Planning} (17\% of time): Brainstorm ideas, organize structure, make notes.
    \item \keyterm{Writing} (60\% of time): Write your first draft quickly, focus on content.
    \item \keyterm{Checking} (23\% of time): Proofread for errors, revise and improve.
\end{enumerate}
\end{vocabbox}

    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Conectores (Linking Words):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Español usa menos conectores
        \item Inglés REQUIERE conectores: However, Moreover, Furthermore, In addition
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

💡 \textbf{Truco:} Cada verbo/adjetivo inglés tiene SU preposición específica. ¡Memorízalas juntas!
\end{spanishbox}

\begin{grammarbox}[title=Error Correction Guide]
\textbf{1. Missing Articles:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Incorrect: I have car.
    \item Correct: I have \textbf{a} car.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{2. Wrong Prepositions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Incorrect: I'm interested for music.
    \item Correct: I'm interested \textbf{in} music.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{3. Run-on Sentences:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Incorrect: I went home I was tired.
    \item Correct: I went home \textbf{because} I was tired.
\end{itemize}
\end{grammarbox}

\section{Linking: Cause, Effect and Contrast}

Adding variety to your writing by using linking words can help improve the flow and coherence of your ideas. Here are some common linking words used to indicate cause, effect, and contrast.

\subsection{Table of Linking Words}
\begin{longtable}{|p{3cm}|p{2.5cm}|p{2cm}|p{5cm}|}
    \hline
    \textbf{Word} & \textbf{Translation} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Example} \\ \hline
    \endhead
    Because & Porque & Cause & I study because I want to learn. \\ \hline
    Therefore & Por lo tanto & Effect & It was raining, therefore we stayed inside. \\ \hline
    However & Sin embargo & Contrast & I am tired; however, I must finish this. \\ \hline
    Whereas & Mientras que & Contrast & He likes apples, whereas she prefers oranges. \\ \hline
    As a result & Como resultado & Effect & He missed the bus; as a result, he was late. \\ \hline
    On the other hand & Por otro lado & Contrast & It's fast. On the other hand, it's expensive. \\ \hline
    Due to & Debido a & Cause & The cancellation was due to the storm. \\ \hline
    Consequently & En consecuencia & Effect & He didn't sleep; consequently, he is tired. \\ \hline
    Although & Aunque & Contrast & Although it was cold, we went for a walk. \\ \hline
    In spite of the fact that & A pesar de que & Contrast & In spite of the fact that it rained, we had fun. \\ \hline
    Nevertheless & No obstante & Contrast & The task was difficult; nevertheless, she completed it. \\ \hline
    While & Mientras & Contrast & He enjoys hiking, while she prefers swimming. \\ \hline
    Despite & A pesar de & Contrast & Despite the challenges, they succeeded. \\ \hline
    Yet & Sin embargo & Contrast & It was raining, yet they went for a walk. \\ \hline
    And & Y & Addition & She likes tea and coffee. \\ \hline
    But & Pero & Contrast & I wanted to go, but I was too tired. \\ \hline
    Because of & Debido a & Cause & The game was canceled because of the rain. \\ \hline
    So & Así que & Effect & It was late, so we went home. \\ \hline
    Even though & A pesar de que & Contrast & Even though it was raining, we went hiking. \\ \hline
\end{longtable}

\subsection{How to Choose Linking Words}

\subsubsection{Cause}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Because:} Introduce a reason. Main clause because + subject + verb.
    \item \textbf{Due to:} Introduce a cause. Due to + noun phrase.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Effect}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Therefore:} Introduce a result. Sentence A. Therefore, Sentence B.
    \item \textbf{As a result:} Describe the consequence. Sentence A. As a result, Sentence B.
    \item \textbf{Consequently:} Show something happens because of the previous statement.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Contrast}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{However:} Contradicts previous statement. Sentence A. However, Sentence B.
    \item \textbf{Whereas/While:} Highlight contrast between two clauses. Clause A, whereas Clause B.
    \item \textbf{On the other hand:} Present alternative perspective.
    \item \textbf{Although / Even though:} Introduce contrast. Although + subject + verb, main clause.
    \item \textbf{Despite / In spite of:} Show contrast. Despite + noun/gerund.
\end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Interested IN} \trans{Interesado en}

\section{Key Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Always plan before writing and leave time for checking.
    \item Watch out for missing articles and wrong prepositions.
    \item Use linking words (However, Therefore) to connect ideas.
    \item Memorize collocations: interested IN, good AT, depend ON.
\end{itemize}
